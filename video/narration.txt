===1===
This child has two changes in one gene, BUB1B. One is a stop signal that lands seven hundred and forty-seven bases too early. The other swaps a single amino acid deep inside the protein's core. Our proposal starts by asking what those two changes actually do, because that determines which treatments could even work.
===2===
The stop variant sits far enough upstream that the cell should destroy that message entirely. Even if it escaped, the protein would lose its whole kinase domain. So that copy contributes nothing. The second change sits buried in a tightly packed core, at high structural confidence. Its neighbours are the same positions where published variants are known to destabilise this protein.
===3===
That leads to the one conclusion the whole proposal rests on. Every functional copy of this protein in this child comes from a single allele that is being degraded too fast. And because this protein is a pseudokinase, the defect was never lost activity. It is lost quantity. Restoring catalysis has no target. Slowing degradation acts on one hundred percent of what he has.
===4===
This is also why we do not propose the answer this field expects. Clearing senescent cells is the famous result in these mice, but it was done genetically, never with a drug, and the one experiment that extended lifespan also significantly increased tumours. In a child who has already had a rhabdomyosarcoma, that barrier is doing useful work. We are not taking it down.
===5===
Ranked first, an NAD plus precursor. It targets the exact acetylation site that marks this protein for degradation, and it has already been given orally to children from age two, for two years, in ataxia telangiectasia, another childhood chromosome instability syndrome, and tolerated. Second, rapamycin, the only actual drug that has rescued an MVA model, published this March. Then metformin, with directly transferable paediatric safety data from Fanconi anaemia. Then JAK inhibition, the most novel angle, and the one we would only pursue if we first saw the inflammation signature in his own cells.
===6===
We do not propose dosing a child first. Step one is his own cells. Measure the protein level, then test whether the compound raises it. Only if it does would we go further, using a trial design already run in Fanconi anaemia children from age two, with micronuclei as the endpoint. Micronuclei are the direct footprint of this disease, and with roughly forty patients worldwide, a cancer incidence endpoint can never be powered.
===7===
We have stated what we do not know. Senescence has never been shown in human MVA cells, and we do not yet know how much protein this child actually has. Both are cheap experiments, and both come before any treatment. That is the proposal. Mechanism first, then the smallest test that could prove us wrong.
